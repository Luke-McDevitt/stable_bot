        Active Stabilization (Ball-Hold)
        (scaffold for v10)
      
      

        Keeps the ball at its current plate-frame position despite
        base perturbations (someone bumps the table, bot on a wobbly
        cart, slow tilt). Combines the existing plate-level loop
        with /base/imu/data feedforward
        and vision feedback. Workspace is limited to ~30 mm of
        top-plate translation; falls back to plate-frame holding
        past that.
        


        Implementation deferred to v10. Math source of truth:
        stewart_vision/_ball_physics.py
        (feedforward_tilt_for_base_accel,
        saturation_fraction; both unit-tested).
        Design: spec §11.7. Reserved mode token:
        BALL_HOLD.

        Limitations to surface for the operator: ~30 mm workspace
        (saturates with large base motion), base-IMU drift over
        long runs, ~25 Hz controller bandwidth. Off-platform
        watchdog still applies.

        Legacy: open-loop rolling ball
        (advanced — superseded by Demo 1)
      
      

        Generates a precomputed tilt-rotation routine. Kept for fallback /
        regression testing only — Demo 1 (Orbit, closed-loop) is the
        primary orbital demo now.
      

      
    
        
  • Ramp-up: spin-up phase. The orbit radius R grows
          0 → R_final via a smoothstep. During this phase the tilt includes
          a tangential component (phase lead) that physically pushes the
          ball along the orbit, building up angular momentum. Shorter =
          more aggressive kick; longer = gentler.
    • 
        
  • Hold cycles: full orbit rotations at the steady-state
          tilt (R̈=Ṙ=0, just centripetal).
    • 
        
  • Ramp-down: mirror of ramp-up. R shrinks R_final → 0 with
          a braking phase lead that drains the ball's tangential velocity
          back to rest.
    • 
        
  • Center hold: flat platform (tilt = 0°) so the ball can
          come to rest on top before the routine ends.
    • 
        
  • Min tilt: safety floor during the active phases (not
          applied during center hold). Usually leave at 0 with the new
          feed-forward; only useful if you see the ball sitting still
          during early ramp-up because of rolling friction.
    • 
      

      

        
        
        
        
        
        
        
        
        
        
        

          Set ramp-up or ramp-down to 0 to disable it (instant jump to/from the hold tilt — useful for breaking stiction but motors will slew hard).
        

      

      
      

        Tip: at R=180 mm the min safe period under the 15°/s cap is ~3 s.
        First try without a ball (do not put a ball on it until you
        like what you see).
      

    


  


  
  


    

      
        System-ID
        empirical Jacobian
      
      

        Per-leg perturbation test. Drives each leg ±δ from rest at
        each Z, samples IMU, builds an empirical 3×6 Jacobian
        (∂rpy/∂leg). Compared to the IK's theoretical Jacobian to
        expose per-leg gain mismatches and asymmetric mounting.
        Level loop must be OFF; drives must be armed in pos mode.

Homing

      

        Two-step workflow. Step 1 stall-homes the bottom endstops
        automatically. Step 2 is a manual top-of-stroke capture —
        you physically move each leg to its top position, then press
        [ENTER] in the prompt to record it.
      


      
      

        

          Pre-homing safety
        

        

          Snapshot the current encoder readings as the rest reference so
          the regular Arm button doesn't refuse (when no
          leg_limits.yaml exists yet) and doesn't
          drive into the endstops. Two-step flow:
        

        
    
          
  1. Manually position each leg to a safe spot (mid-stroke).
    2. 
          
  2. Click 📍 Capture rest below — just records positions, doesn't arm.
    3. 
          
  3. Then press the regular Arm button in the left column.
    4. 
        

        

          
          
        

        

      


      
      

        

          Manual endstop capture (recommended)
        

        

          No motor movement. Pick which legs to capture (you can do them
          one-by-one or in groups — useful when the platform is unstable
          with all legs at the same extreme), capture BOTTOM and/or TOP,
          press Save. Captures merge with existing
          leg_limits.yaml — already-saved legs
          aren't clobbered when you save a new group.
